As of Nov 25, 2026, a federal jury in Brooklyn has found Huawei guilty on 10 of 12 criminal counts. The verdict is split, and the part that was rejected is the part prosecutors framed as the biggest. This post summarizes what two outlets, [BBC News](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cmy49ngydqzpo) and [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1041820/huawei-guilty-trade-secrets-trial), report about it. It is a jury verdict, not a final outcome.

## What the jury decided

BBC News reports that Huawei was found guilty of conspiracy to steal trade secrets and of bank fraud, after a two-month trial. It was found not guilty of the overarching racketeering conspiracy charge, which BBC describes as requiring jurors to find the company acted like a criminal enterprise.

The Verge gives a longer list of the guilty counts: money laundering, violating US sanctions, bank fraud, wire fraud and conspiracy to defraud the US. It also reports an acquittal on obstruction of justice, alongside the racketeering acquittal. The Verge says the case accused Huawei of stealing source code and proprietary technology from several companies, including T-Mobile, Cisco and Motorola.

## What happens next

Per The Verge, sentencing is set for September 14th, 2027. It quotes the Department of Justice as saying Huawei may face "financial penalties including criminal fines, forfeiture, and restitution."

Both outlets report that Huawei intends to fight. The Verge says a Huawei spokesperson told NBC News the charges are "without legal or factual merit" and that the company plans to appeal. BBC says Huawei called the racketeering acquittal proof that "these allegations lack factual basis", and that its defence lawyer expects an appeal and "significant advocacy" for the lowest sentence possible.

## The two sides of the same verdict

BBC quotes a US Attorney General statement that Huawei and three subsidiaries "conducted their business in the United States on the basis of lies and theft". Defence attorney Brian Heberlig told BBC the racketeering count, which alleged a corporate culture of committing crime, was "a significant overreach" that the jury "soundly rejected", while also saying the defence had "mixed emotions about the rest of the verdict".

The Verge notes the background: the US government blacklisted Huawei in 2019 over national security concerns, and the case grew out of the 2018 arrest of its CFO, Wanzhou Meng.

## What this doesn't say

- The sources don't say what the fines or any other penalty will be. That is a September 2027 question.
- Neither source says what the verdict means for Huawei's customers, suppliers or product availability, so this post doesn't guess.
- The two outlets list the counts slightly differently. This post uses each one's own wording and the shared figure of 10 of 12.
- An appeal is expected by Huawei, and it hasn't been filed as of the sources' publication.

What I'd take from it: the headline "guilty" and the headline "cleared of racketeering" are both accurate, and the sentencing date is the next thing worth watching.
