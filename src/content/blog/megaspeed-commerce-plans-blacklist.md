As of Nov 24, 2026, the US Commerce Department says it plans to add Megaspeed Ltd, a Singapore-based data center developer, to a list of entities that are barred from buying or selling goods in the US. Its subsidiary Speedmatrix is also being added, according to the [BBC's report](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cv98xpg5dg5zo), published Nov 23. This post covers only what that report says, and the allegations in it are allegations.

## What was announced

The BBC says Commerce announced the plan but did not say when the ban takes effect. The BBC has asked Commerce, the White House and Megaspeed for comment.

Commerce says it has been investigating Megaspeed for almost two years. It says it suspected the company of violating US export controls by helping get Nvidia's chips to companies in China, which has been blocked from buying that technology since 2022.

## Why a buyer matters

The BBC describes Megaspeed as a known buyer of billions of dollars of Nvidia chips, including through Speedmatrix. It says Megaspeed is based in Singapore but has significant business in China, and that Alibaba has been reported to be one of its largest customers.

That is the practical part for anyone who plans GPU capacity. An export rule aimed at one country can land on a third-country intermediary. The BBC says the scrutiny sharpened after media reports that a former Megaspeed executive was accused of working as an asset for Beijing. China denies that she is a spy and has warned the US not to use the matter as a pretext for a technological blockade.

## What the report does not say

- No effective date for the ban.
- Nothing on what a listing would require of Megaspeed's customers, such as whether existing contracts or hosted workloads are affected.
- Nothing confirming the funneling. The BBC says Commerce suspected it, and Megaspeed's response is not in the report.

## What I'd take from it

If you rent GPU capacity from a provider outside the US, it is worth knowing who that provider buys from and where it sells. A supplier named in an export investigation is a supply risk even before any rule takes effect. That is my reading of the report, not a claim made in it.
