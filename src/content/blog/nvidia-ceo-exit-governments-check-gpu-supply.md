As of Nov 15, 2026, several governments responded to Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang's exit by checking on chip supply and local projects. Two outlets, [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/14/asian-governments-react-to-nvidia-ceo-jensen-huangs-exit/) and [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1033781/nvidia-jensen-huang-last-visionary-showman-founder-ceo), both published on Nov 14, report on what it means for people who buy or build on Nvidia hardware. This post only covers that supply side, and every claim below is attributed to the outlet that made it.

## What happened

TechCrunch reports that Huang resigned on Thursday, Nov 12, and that CFO Colette Kress is interim CEO while Nvidia searches for a permanent replacement. TechCrunch also reports that Nvidia's board opened an independent investigation on Nov 8, and that Nvidia's stock fell sharply through the week. This post does not repeat or assess the allegations around that investigation, which are still allegations.

## What governments are saying about supply

TechCrunch's Asia reporter, Kate Park, surveyed the reactions:

- **South Korea.** The Science Ministry said Nvidia reaffirmed its plan to supply 260,000 advanced GPUs to the country and to continue joint AI projects. The government's AI strategy council also held an emergency meeting on Friday, per a local media report cited by TechCrunch.
- **Taiwan.** Taipei's city government said Nvidia's planned overseas headquarters there remains on track, and that the company has met its contractual obligations so far.
- **Japan.** The Industry Ministry said it would closely monitor Nvidia. Deputy chief cabinet secretary Masanao Ozaki said it was too early to assess the impact on the global AI industry, and stressed Japan's commitment to a stable semiconductor supply.
- **China.** According to Reuters, as relayed by TechCrunch, Huang's exit raised uncertainty over more than $3 billion in Chinese AI computing projects that rely on Nvidia's restricted processors, many of which are subject to U.S. export controls.

## What analysts say about the roadmap

The Verge's Sean Hollister collected analyst reactions. Pat Moorhead of Moor Insights & Strategy told The Verge: "This is a stock shock, not a business shock, for the next year. The roadmap and manufacturing are locked in through 2027." He named a different risk: "customers hedging during a vacuum."

Ben Bajarin of Creative Strategies said the supply position "does not walk out the door with one person," pointing to wafers, packaging, memory and rack-level partners. IDC's Tom Mainelli said choosing a replacement will be key and "there will undoubtedly be bumps along the way."

The Verge also reports that Huang had no named successor, and that Nvidia is around 6 percent of the S&P 500 by itself.

## What I'd take from it

For a team with a GPU order in flight, the reporting is reassuring on the near term: one government says Nvidia reaffirmed its commitment, others say projects are on track or supply is a priority, and the analysts quoted expect the product plan to hold. The open question, per Moorhead, is whether Nvidia keeps inventing at the same pace once the locked-in plans run out, and whether customers hedge in the meantime.

## Limits

- The 260,000 GPU figure is the South Korean ministry's account of what Nvidia told it. TechCrunch does not quote Nvidia directly, and neither outlet gives a delivery schedule.
- The analysts are outside commentators giving forecasts, not Nvidia. Their view that nothing changes strategically is an opinion.
- The story is moving. Treat everything here as true as of Nov 15, 2026, and check for newer reporting before acting.
