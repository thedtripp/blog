As of Nov 16, 2026, Apple has opened enrollment for a study of how people use conversational AI for health questions. [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1033355/apples-new-study-looks-to-improve-the-effectiveness-of-ai-health-tools) and [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/15/apple-launches-new-app-to-study-how-people-use-ai-for-health/) both report it, and every claim below is attributed to the outlet that made it. The two outlets disagree on one detail, covered below.

## What the study is

The Verge reports that Apple is running the Apple Health AI Usage Study with OpenEvidence, which The Verge describes as a clinical AI platform used by healthcare professionals. Participants use a new iOS app to ask medical questions and then rate the answers on accuracy, detail and relevance, according to The Verge.

TechCrunch reports that answers are grounded in clinical sources, including The New England Journal of Medicine, the Journal of the American Medical Association and Nature. It adds that participants will also take monthly surveys, and that Apple aims to run the study with up to 200,000 people for more than 12 months. Both outlets say the app is available to download now.

## Who can join

Both outlets say participants must be in the U.S. and over 21, and must run iOS 26 or later. They disagree on the hardware and on Apple Intelligence:

- The Verge: the app requires an iPhone 15 Pro, and it doesn't use Apple Intelligence or integrate with Siri.
- TechCrunch: the app requires an iPhone 15 or later, with Apple Intelligence enabled.

This post can't settle that. If you plan to enroll, check the App Store listing.

## What happens to the data

The Verge reports that participants can optionally share Apple HealthKit data, such as activity, sleep or clinical lab results, so answers include more context. It says Apple states the data won't be used to train AI models.

TechCrunch reports that Apple said data will be anonymized before it goes to a secure third-party cloud provider, and that OpenEvidence deletes query and response data after 24 hours.

## What neither outlet says

- Neither says whether Apple plans to bring AI health answers to its Health app or Siri. TechCrunch says that isn't clear.
- Neither reports results. This is the start of a study, and the outlets report Apple's stated aims.
- The privacy claims are Apple's own, as relayed by the outlets. Neither outlet reports an independent check.

What I'd take from it: Apple is collecting ratings on answer quality before it says anything about shipping a product. That is a reading of the reports, not something either outlet states.
