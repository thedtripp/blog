As of Nov 18, 2026, Nvidia says it has stopped shipping its H200 chips to China. Interim CEO Colette Kress said so on Tuesday's earnings call, according to both [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/17/nvidia-is-abandoning-china-after-jensen-huangs-ouster/) and [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1036445/nvidia-h200-ai-chips-china-stop-selling). This post covers only what both reported, and it attributes each claim to the outlet that made it.

## What was said

The H200 is a chip from Nvidia's Hopper generation. The Verge quotes Kress from the call: guidance "does not have any licensed Hopper data center products included," and Nvidia is "no longer shipping" those products to China. TechCrunch puts it as Nvidia no longer selling the H200 in China, ending its part in what TechCrunch calls a loophole in the US ban on selling advanced chips there.

That arrangement was one Jensen Huang pushed for. TechCrunch says it was brokered by Huang and President Trump. The Verge says Huang fought for US approval and that the White House gave it in December of last year. Huang stepped down as CEO last week, and Kress is now the acting CEO.

## China was already holding the chips back

TechCrunch says China had largely refused to accept delivery even before Nvidia stopped shipping. The Verge, citing Reuters, says around 600,000 H200 chips were stuck in Hong Kong after Beijing withheld approval for large-scale imports. Both tie that to Beijing's push to rely less on US chips.

## What I'd take from it

Read this way, US approval to sell and chips actually arriving are two separate things, and by the time Nvidia stopped, the second had already stalled. For a team that plans GPU capacity around export rules, the lesson is to track where chips are physically cleared, not only where a license exists. That is my reading of the two reports, not a claim from either one.

## Limits

- The Verge says Kress did not explain why Nvidia stopped. TechCrunch's headline frames the move as coming after Huang's ouster, but that is timing, not a stated cause.
- Neither report says whether Nvidia plans other business with China. Neither gives a number for how much revenue the H200 line was expected to bring in.
- TechCrunch reports more, including new US export rules and analyst estimates of the revenue effect. Those appear in only one outlet, so they are not in this post.
- The story is moving. Treat everything here as true as of Nov 18, 2026, and check for newer reporting before acting.

*This post summarizes TechCrunch and The Verge reporting. I have not read Nvidia's earnings call transcript myself.*
