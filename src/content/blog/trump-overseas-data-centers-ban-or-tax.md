At a press conference on Friday, Nov 20, President Trump said US companies will not be allowed to build data centers outside the country. In the same answer he said that if they do, "we're going to put a tremendous tax on them." Those are two different policies, and as of Nov 21 there is no text behind either one. This post sticks to what two news outlets reported.

## What was said

[The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/policy/1038892/trump-foreign-data-centers-tax) published a transcript of the key remarks. Trump said: "We're not going to allow our companies to build data centers outside of our country. We're not going to allow that to happen." A few sentences later: "If they do that, they can, but we're going to put a tremendous tax on them." He said China "will take them all" if the US allows it.

[The BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cmde1lw9y54ko) headlined the same remarks as a threatened ban on US firms building AI data centres overseas, and also quotes the "tremendous tax" line. The two outlets frame it differently because Trump's words support both readings.

## How little detail there is

Both outlets note that Trump gave few specifics. By The Verge's account, Trump said he had not told his own team: "I haven't even said that to my people. But I'm telling them now." The BBC adds that it remains unclear how such a policy would be enforced if it were brought in, and that it is unclear whether the idea will become policy at all.

The Verge also points out that a new Democratic-majority Congress is due to be sworn in within a couple of months, which could make the promise hard to fulfill.

## Which projects exist abroad

The BBC lists projects that sit outside the US today:

- Google announced a €13bn ($15bn) investment in AI infrastructure in Finland in September, including three data centres. Last month, building work on two of them was halted over concerns about clearing more than 300 hectares of forest. Trump called out the Finland build specifically, saying his administration was "not allowing that to happen."
- Meta has broken ground on a data centre in Alberta, Canada.
- Anthropic has signed a long-term agreement to lease a proposed data centre in Nova Scotia.

The BBC says it contacted Meta and Google for comment. Its article says Washington also announced earlier in the week that firms would need a licence to buy advanced chips, so officials can monitor whether they serve Chinese companies.

## Limits: what this doesn't say

Neither outlet reports a draft, a statute, an executive order or an agency. There is no rate for the tax, no definition of "building" a data center abroad, and no word on projects already under way. Whether this is a ban, a tax or neither is open. I'd avoid treating it as a rule until text exists.

## What I'd take from it

For someone planning capacity, there is nothing to act on today. The thing to watch is which of the two statements turns into a document, and whether it covers projects that are already financed. Until then it's one remark at a press conference, reported consistently by two outlets.
