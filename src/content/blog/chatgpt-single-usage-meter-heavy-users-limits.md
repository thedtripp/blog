As of Nov 26, 2026, OpenAI has started rolling out a combined Chat and Work experience in ChatGPT to a small group of Pro subscribers, according to [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/25/openai-says-heavy-chatgpt-users-may-hit-limits-sooner-as-it-merges-chat-and-work/). The part that matters to people who use it all day is the meter. This post covers only what that report says.

## What changed

Chat and Work will share a single usage meter across ChatGPT, an OpenAI spokesperson told TechCrunch. Some subscribers have criticized the merger on the grounds that it could restrict their usage.

OpenAI product lead Thibault "Tibo" Sottiaux announced the first rollout in a post on X on Tuesday. According to TechCrunch, OpenAI plans to bring shared usage limits more broadly in December, per its support page.

## Who feels it

The spokesperson said most users are not expected to face more frequent usage resets, but that a small fraction of the heaviest users may hit their limits sooner. OpenAI did not say, in what TechCrunch reports, how large that fraction is or where the threshold sits.

Conversations that use the Instant model are the exception. They remain unlimited and do not count toward the shared allowance, and users can keep using Instant after they run out of their usage limit. The spokesperson added that OpenAI will keep working on efficiency so subscribers can get more out of their plans.

## What I'd take from it

If you lean on ChatGPT for long working sessions, the thing to watch is whether chat and work tasks now draw down one shared pool. Whether that applies to you depends on how you use it, and the report gives no numbers to check against. That is my reading of the report, not a claim from OpenAI or TechCrunch.

## Limits

- The rollout is limited so far. The report describes a small group of Pro subscribers, with a broader rollout planned for December.
- The report gives no limit values, no definition of "heaviest users," and no figure for how much sooner anyone would hit a limit.
- The claim that most users won't notice is OpenAI's own, relayed by a spokesperson. TechCrunch does not independently measure it.
- This is moving. Treat everything here as true as of Nov 26, 2026, and check OpenAI's support page for current limits.

*This post summarizes TechCrunch's reporting. I have not tested the new limits myself.*
